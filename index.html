§ 03

Compliance

Our flagship practice.

We don’t issue SOC 2 reports. Independent CPA firms do. We get you ready and run the process with them.

SOC 2 and HIPAA, without a security team.

Enterprise customers and investors want proof you take security seriously. Compliance gets done when someone owns it. We own it, from choosing the tools to closing out the audit, and we have done it at SaaS companies with no dedicated security team.

The SOC 2 ledger What gets done, and what you walk away with.
No. The work You walk away with Status
01 Plan. Pick the scope, the trust criteria and the compliance platform. A plan with owners and dates.
02 Policies. Write the policies: security, access, change management, risk, continuity. Policies that match how your team really works.
03 Controls. Put access controls, audit logging and change management in place in your cloud. Controls mapped to real systems, not a spreadsheet.
04 Automation. Roll out Vanta to monitor controls and collect evidence across your cloud accounts. Evidence that collects itself.
05 Continuity. Design and validate disaster recovery for your platform. A recovery plan built for your real architecture.
06 Audit. Hire the auditor, act as their main contact, and drive every finding to closure. Findings closed on time.
07 Handoff. Document the recurring work and train the people who will own it. We stay on call for the next audit cycle. A program your team runs day to day.

Building for healthcare?

We plan HIPAA readiness the same way. We map the Security Rule’s administrative, physical and technical safeguards to your systems, find the gaps, and hand you a prioritized plan your engineers can work from.

§ 04

How we work

Business first, technology second. On our first website in 2003, and still true.

Get clear. Stay focused. Architecture is the plan.

Most surprises in software come from fuzzy goals and plans nobody can see. So we get clear on the business goal, keep the work focused on it, and draw the system before we commit to a date. Then we tell you where things stand in plain business terms.

  1. A written plan

    One page: the goal, what’s in, what’s out, the risks and the date. Agreed before work starts.

  2. Estimates from the architecture

    We sketch the system first, then estimate from the sketch. Every date comes with its reasons.

  3. Tradeoffs you can decide on

    When there is a choice, you get the options with their cost, time and risk, plus our recommendation.

  4. Status in plain language

    A short, regular note: what’s done, what’s next, what’s at risk, and what we need from you.

  5. Small releases, shipped often

    Small, meaningful releases beat a big reveal. You see progress as it happens.
